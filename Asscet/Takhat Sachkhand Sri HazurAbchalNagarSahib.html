About Hazur Sahib

Hazur Sahib (Hazūrī Sāhib; lit. 'presence of the sahib/master'), also known as Takht Sachkhand Sri Hazur Abchalnagar Sahib, is one of the five takhts in Sikhism. The gurdwara was built between 1832 and 1837 by Nasir-ud-Daulah, Asaf Jah IV upon the request of his friend Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1780–1839). It is located on the banks of the Godavari River at the city of Nanded in the state of Maharashtra, India.

The structure is built at the place where Guru Gobind Singh Ji left his earthly life. The inner room of the gurdwara, known as the Angitha Sahib, is a vault built over the place where Guru Gobind Singh Ji was cremated in 1708.

History & Significance

The Final Days

Hazur Sahib marks the site where Guru Gobind Singh Ji had his camp in 1708, held his court, merged into the Primal (Joti Jot), and declared the Guru Granth Sahib as his eternal successor.

Construction & Architecture

The present building was constructed in the early 1830s using funds, artisans, and labor sent from Punjab by Maharaja Ranjit Singh.

Dual Sanctum Sanctorum

Unlike other shrines, it features two sanctum rooms: an outer room for regular functions and an inner high-security vault housing priceless weapons and personal belongings of the Guru.

One of Five Takhts

It holds primary importance as one of the five supreme temporal seats (Takhts) of authority in Sikhism, celebrating its grand tricentennial in October 2008.

Unique Traditions & Legacy

  • Ancient Customs: Preserves ancient traditions practiced during the time of the Gurus, such as applying a sandal-wood tilak on the foreheads of priests and devotees.
  • Holy Scriptures: The Takht houses both the Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji and the Sri Dasam Granth, following the pattern of Takht Sri Patna Sahib.
  • Kirtan Maryada: Known for rich musical traditions upheld by eminent Ragis, such as Bhai Jagat Singh who served devotedly from 1934 to 1978.
