Pandavleni Caves, Nashik

Back to Nashik

About Pandavleni Caves

Location: Buddha Vihar, Pathardi Phata, Nashik, Maharashtra 422010

A 2000-year-old group of 24 rock-cut caves, also known as the Trirashmi Caves, carved between the 3rd century BC and the 2nd century AD. These historic caves depict tales of the three ancient dynasties that once ruled Nashik—the Kshatrapas, the Satavahanas, and the Abhiras. Since all the caves face eastwards, an early morning visit is highly recommended to witness sunlight falling directly on the exquisite carvings.

Major attractions include the main prayer hall (Chaitya) housing a beautiful Stupa, and Cave No. 10, which features complete structural integrity and extensive historical inscriptions.

History & Heritage

Origin of Name

Originally known as 'Pundru' (meaning yellow ochre in Pali) because Buddhist monks wore yellow robes ('chivara'). The name Pandavleni is a misnomer with no historical connection to the Mahabharata Pandavas.

Religious Significance

Served as a peaceful residential and meditation space for Buddhist monks and Jain saints like Ambika Devi, Tirthankara Vrishabdeo, and Veer Manibhadraji.

How to Reach Pandavleni Caves

  • By Road: Nashik is well-connected by major highways across Maharashtra. Local auto-rickshaws are easily available from the city to reach the caves at Pathardi Phata.
  • By Train: Accessible via Nashik Road Railway Station, which connects to major cities like Mumbai, Pune, and Thane. Auto-rickshaws run regularly from the station to the caves.
  • By Air: The nearest airport is Gandhinagar Airport in Nashik. Taxis and autos are available from the airport directly to the location.

Best Time to Visit

The ideal time to visit Pandavleni Caves is from July to February, when the surrounding landscape's scenic beauty is vastly enhanced by pleasant weather and lush greenery.
