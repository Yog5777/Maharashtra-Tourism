About Muktidham Temple

Muktidham is a prominent white-marble temple complex located in Nashik. Built in 1971 by local industrialist Mr. J.D. Chauhan-Bytco, it is famous for housing exact replicas of all 12 Jyotirlingas. The temple walls feature inscriptions from the Mahabharata and the life of Lord Krishna (the main deity), along with all eighteen chapters of the Holy Geeta carved meticulously onto the walls.

The complex also showcases idols of major Hindu gods and goddesses including Vishnu, Lakshmi, Rama, Lakshmana, Sita, Hanuman, Durga, and Ganesha, alongside a special room featuring notable paintings by Raghubir Mulgaonkar and a Dharamshala that accommodates at least 200 pilgrims.

Key Highlights

12 Jyotirlingas

Exact replicas of the original holy Jyotirlingas of India built inside the temple complex.

Holy Geeta Carvings

Intricate carving of all 18 chapters of the sacred Bhagavad Geeta across the temple walls.

Art & Paintings

Beautiful wall murals depicting scenes from Lord Krishna's life and renowned paintings by Raghubir Mulgaonkar.

Spiritual Belief

According to Hindu legends, visiting this complex is equivalent to the 'Chaar Dhaam Yatra' and grants salvation (mukti).

How to Reach

  • By Air: The nearest airport is Ozar Airport, situated close to Nashik.
  • By Train: Accessible via the Nashik Road Railway Station.
  • By Road: Located 8 km away from the Nashik bus stop, local transports like autos, cabs, and taxis are easily available from anywhere in the city.

Best Time to Visit

The temple can be visited year-round, but it is especially vibrant during the Kumbh Mela (held every 12 years) and major Hindu festivals. The annual Saryu Mahotsava Festival celebrated in December is also a magnificent time to experience the grandeur of the complex.
