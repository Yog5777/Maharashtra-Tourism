About Sinhagad Fort

Sinhagad (Lion's Fort) is a historic hill fortress located about 35 km southwest of Pune, India. Perched on an isolated cliff of the Bhuleswar range in the Sahyadri Mountains, it stands roughly 760 meters above ground and 1,312 meters above mean sea level.

Originally known as Kondhana, archaeological clues like ancient caves and carvings in the Kaundinyeshwar temple suggest parts of the site could be up to 2,000 years old.

Strategic Design & Architecture

Natural Defenses

Strategically built with very steep slopes for natural protection, featuring walls and bastions positioned only at key access points.

Fort Gates

Features two primary entry points: the Kalyan Darwaza to the southeast and the Pune Darwaza to the northeast.

The Legendary Battle of Sinhagad (1670)

One of the most glorious chapters in the fort's history occurred in March 1670 when Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's trusted military general, Tanaji Malusare, recaptured the fort from the Mughal forces.

  • The Midnight Scale: Tanaji and his men stealthily scaled a steep, sheer cliff at night using a tamed monitor lizard locally known as a Ghorpad (Yashwant).
  • Fierce Combat: A brutal battle ensued against the Mughal garrison headed by Rajput Sardar Udaybhan Singh Rathod. Tanaji lost his life in the clash, after which his brother Suryaji took command and successfully secured the fort.
  • "Gad Ala, Pan Sinha Gela": Deeply grieved upon hearing of Tanaji's supreme sacrifice, Shivaji Maharaj famously remarked, "The fort is conquered, but the lion was lost," leading to the fort's renaming to Sinhagad.

Timeline & Historical Shifts

Over centuries, Kondhana/Sinhagad changed hands multiple times among the Koli kings, Bahmanis, Adil Shahis, Mughals, and the Maratha Empire. It served as a vital administrative and strategic anchor alongside neighboring forts like Rajgad, Purandar, and Torna. Notably, Peshwa ruler Rajaram I passed away inside the fort on March 3, 1700 AD. The Maratha rule persisted until the British captured it in 1818.
