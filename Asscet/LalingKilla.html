About Laling Killa

Laling Killa (Marathi: लळिंग किल्ला) is a historical hill fort situated in the Dhule district of Maharashtra, perched atop the Laling hill approximately 9.65 km south of Dhule city. During the medieval era under the Faruqi Kings of Khandesh, it served as one of the region's most prominent strategic strongholds.

Positioned along the historic Surat-Burhanpur commercial trade route, the fort currently lies mostly in ruins due to natural weathering and historical military conflicts, yet it offers stunning panoramic views of the surrounding landscape and settlements below.

Historical Significance & Features

Faruqi Dynasty Capital

Built during the reign of the first Faruqi king, Malik Raja (1370–1399), who designated Laling as his chief fort rather than Thalner, passing it to his eldest son.

Historical Sieges

In 1437, Nasir Khan and Miran Adil Khan were besieged here by a Bahamani general until relieved by reinforcements advancing from Gujarat.

Ancient Structures

Contains remnants of functional rock-cut cisterns, weaponry ruins, a semi-circular dam structure, and a proximate pavilion (Chatri).

Scenic Panoramas

Acts as an ideal vantage point offering breathtaking, sweeping views of the plains and human settlements below the citadel.

How to Reach & Trek Information

  • Location Access: Located about 16 km west of the Dhule Central Bus Station (CBS), easily accessible from the busy Mumbai-Agra NH3 highway.
  • Trek Route: The trail begins near an old Hemadpanthi temple in the base village. After climbing halfway up, carved stone steps lead directly to the summit.
  • Difficulty Level: The climb is very easy and gentle, making it accessible for casual trekkers and history enthusiasts alike.
