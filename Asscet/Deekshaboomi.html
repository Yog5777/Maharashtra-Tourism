About Deekshabhoomi

Deekshabhoomi is a sacred monument of Navayana Buddhism located at Nagpur city in Maharashtra state of India; where Dr. B. R. Ambedkar embraced Buddhism with approximately 600,000 of his followers on Ashoka Vijaya Dashami, 14 October 1956. Dr. Ambedkar revived Buddhism in India, and his conversion is deeply significant for millions of people. Today, the largest stupa in Asia is erected in his memory at this historic site.

Significance & Identity

Deekshabhoomi is regarded as a sacred place and Prernabhoomi (inspiring land) of social revolution against discrimination and inequality. It is the first pilgrimage center of Ambedkarite Buddhism in India. Millions of pilgrims visit Deekshabhoomi every year, especially on Dhamma Chakra Pravartan Din (Samrat Ashoka Vijaya Dashami) and 14 October.

Deeksha literally means 'act of ordaining' and bhoomi means the 'ground'. Thus, Deekshabhoomi means the ground where people got ordained as Buddhists. This religious mass conversion at one place was the first ever of its kind in history.

History

Dr. Ambedkar had declared in 1935 that although he was born as a Hindu, he would not die as one, as conversion was the solution to abandon the caste system. After exhaustively studying major world religions, he chose Buddhism for himself and his followers.

He selected Nagpur for his conversion ceremony because Nagpur was the historical land of the 'Nag' people who had embraced and propagated Buddhism in its early period. On 14 October 1956, Dr. Ambedkar and his followers took the oath of Three Jewels, Five Precepts, and 22 Vows from the Burmese monk Mahasthavir Chandramani, making Nagpur the birthplace of the Neo-Buddhist movement.

Following Dr. Ambedkar's demise, the 'Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Deekshabhoomi Smarak Samiti' was formed to manage the site and construct a grand stupa as a memorial for the historic ceremony.

Architecture

Deekshabhoomi is spread over four acres of central land in Nagpur. The stupa was designed by architect Sheo Dan Mal. Construction of the stupa started in July 1978 and was officially inaugurated on 18 December 2001 by the President of India, K. R. Narayanan.

Location & Map

Deekshabhoomi is located centrally in Nagpur and is easily accessible by buses, auto-rickshaws, and taxis from the railway station and airport.
