About Lonar Lake

Located in the Buldhana district of Maharashtra, Lonar Lake (or Lonar Crater) is a notified National Geo-heritage Monument, and a saline-alkaline soda lake created by a meteorite collision impact during the Pleistocene Epoch. It is one of the only four known hyper-velocity impact craters in basaltic rock anywhere on Earth.

The crater has a mean diameter of 1.2 km and sits 137 meters below its rim, which spans about 1.8 km. Recognized globally by scientific bodies like the Smithsonian Institution and USGS, a 2019 IIT Bombay study revealed that its soil minerals closely match moon rocks brought back during the Apollo Program. In November 2020, it was designated as a protected Ramsar site.

Scientific & Geographical Features

Lunar Connection

Mineral composition in the crater soil matches lunar samples from the Apollo mission.

Ancient Heritage

Mentioned in ancient scriptures like Skanda Purana and surrounded by historic Hemadpanti/Yadava temples.

Rich Ecosystem

Hosts a diverse array of resident and migratory birds, unique flora, and dense surrounding tree-savannah vegetation.

Protected Ramsar Site

Recognized internationally and nationally for its rare geological and ecological significance.

Conservation Challenges

  • Water Level Changes: Studies indicate water reduction is tied to nearby percolation dam changes and blocked feeder streams.
  • Pollution & Tourism: Unregulated tourist activities, agricultural runoff, and religious festivals pose ongoing threats to the delicate ecosystem.
  • Preservation Efforts: Ongoing civic initiatives like "Save Lonar" continue to advocate for tighter environmental safeguards.
