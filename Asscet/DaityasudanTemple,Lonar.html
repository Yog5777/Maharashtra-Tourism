About Daityasudan Temple

Located in the Buldhana district of Maharashtra, the Daityasudan Temple is a magnificent medieval shrine dedicated to Lord Vishnu. Built during the Chalukya Dynasty (between the 6th and 12th centuries), it stands as a brilliant architectural masterpiece designed in the Hemadpanthi style and constructed in the form of an irregular star.

The temple is renowned for its intricate stone carvings, which bear striking similarities to the famous Khajuraho temples of Madhya Pradesh. Measuring 105 feet long and 84.5 feet broad with an elevated plinth of about 1.5 meters, it remains an archaeological marvel and a major center of historical and religious significance.

Architectural & Sculptural Highlights

Chalukya & Hemadpanthi Design

Built as an irregular star layout featuring exquisite exterior wall sculptures and detailed ceiling carvings.

Three Chamber Layout

Comprises the sanctum sanctorum (Garbhagriha), the Antaral (puja hall), and the Sabhamandap for group offerings.

Puranic Story Carvings

Portrays legendary tales including Lord Krishna defeating Lavanasur, Narasimha and Hiranyakashyap, and Rasa Krida.

Unique Metallic Idol

The main deity of Lord Vishnu standing atop Lavanasur is crafted from an ore with exceptionally high metal content.

Mythological History

  • The Legend of Lonasur: According to ancient lore, a powerful demon named Lonasur (or Lavanasur) resided in this area with his sisters and terrorized the locals before being vanquished by Lord Vishnu in his Daityasudan avatar.
  • Original Dedication: Based on the standing image of Lord Surya in the principal niche at the back, historians conjecture that the temple was originally dedicated to the Sun God before becoming a prominent Vaishnavite shrine.
