About Akola Fort

Akola Fort (also known as Asadgad), along with the Narnala and Akot forts, forms the major fortifications of the Akola district in Maharashtra, India. Initially, the fort was built as a mud structure by Akol Singh during the reign of Aurangzeb. Legend says Akol Singh saw a hare chasing a dog and, considering it a holy sign, constructed a fort around the village. Later, it was heavily reconstructed using large granite stone blocks, lime stones, mud blocks, clay bricks, wood, iron, and mortar, making it both strong and architecturally impressive.

History

Akola was heavily fortified in 1697 CE during the reign of Aurangzeb by Asad Khan, from whom the fort took its name (Asadgad). During the era of Chhatrapati Sambhaji Maharaj, the Maratha army chased Mughal troops up to Akola and Murtizapur to assert control and protect the local population. In 1803, Arthur Wellesley camped here before proceeding to win the Battle of Argaon in the Second Anglo-Maratha War. The fortress was dismantled by the British Raj in about 1870. Reports from 1910 indicate that the central part of the fort (the Hawakhana) was used as a school.

Major Features

Akola Fort is notable in that it is bereft of excessive decorative embellishments, emphasizing functionality and strength. There are several historical inscriptions found across the fort:

  • Dahi Handa Gate: Mentions its construction date as 1114 AH (1697 CE) during the reign of Emperor Aurangzeb and Minister Nawab Asad Khan.
  • Fateh Buruj Bastion: Mentions Minister Asad Khan and Emperor Shah Alam.
  • Eidgah: Mentions completion by Khawja Abdul Latif in 1116 AH (1698 CE).
  • Agarves Gate: Features an inscription in Marathi stating that Govind Appaji constructed the fort in 1843 CE.

Shri Raj Rajeshwar Mandir

Located within the city near the fort area, Akola's oldest Shiva temple is the Shri Raj Rajeshwar Mandir. This ancient Shiv temple holds deep spiritual value for the entire district.

Folklore & Local Legends

A famous story is associated with the Raj Rajeshwar temple. It is said that King Akolsingh's queen worshipped Lord Shiva at midnight. Suspecting her motives, the king secretly followed her with a sword. Realizing her husband doubted her character, the heartbroken queen prayed to Lord Shiva for proof of her innocence. Legend says the Shiva Linga opened up, allowed the queen inside, and closed back with a visible crack. Realizing his mistake, the king was overcome with remorse. To this day, the Shiva Linga in this temple retains a small crack corroborating this legend.
