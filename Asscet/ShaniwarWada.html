About Shaniwar Wada

Shaniwarwada is a historical fortification in the city of Pune in Maharashtra, India. Built in 1732, it served as the great seat of the Peshwas of the Maratha Empire until 1818, becoming the center of Indian politics in the 18th century.

While largely destroyed by an unexplained fire in 1828, its surviving structures and stone base are meticulously maintained as a premier tourist site.

Construction & Architecture

Foundation & Cost

Peshwa Baji Rao I laid its foundation on Saturday, 10 January 1730. Completed in 1732 at a total cost of Rs. 16,110, its opening ceremony took place on 22 January 1732.

Materials Used

Teak was imported from Junnar jungles, stone from Chinchwad quarries, and mineral lime from Jejuri. Originally planned in stone, the upper storeys were built in brick following orders from the Chhatrapati King Shahu.

Historical Milestones & Legends

  • Thriving Population: By 1758, at least a thousand people lived inside the fort complex.
  • The Tragedy of Narayanrao: In 1773, the young ruling Peshwa Narayanrao was assassinated here. Popular local legend says his ghost still cries out "Kaka mala vachava" (Uncle, save me) on full moon nights.
  • The Great Fire of 1828: On 27 February 1828, a devastating fire raged for seven days, leaving behind only the heavy granite ramparts, strong teak gateways, and deep foundations.
