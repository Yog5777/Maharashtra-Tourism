About Dnyanganga Wildlife Sanctuary

Spanning 205 square kilometers in the Buldhana district, the Dnyanganga Wildlife Sanctuary is situated 28 km from Buldhana and 20 km from Khamgaon. Located near the Dnyanganga River (a tributary of the Tapti River), it features an undulating terrain interspersed with gentle hillocks and two scenic lakes.

Managed by the Office of the Field Director, Melghat Tiger Reserve (Maharashtra Forest Department), the sanctuary serves as a rich natural habitat for diverse wildlife and nearly 150 species of birds.

Wildlife & Biodiversity Highlights

Predatory Fauna

Home to leopards, sloth bears, hyenas, jungle cats, and jackals.

Herbivores

Supports robust populations of barking deer, blue bulls (nilgai), and spotted deer.

Avian Diversity

Hosts around 150 resident and migratory bird species, making it a paradise for birdwatchers.

Best Time to Visit

Open year-round; peak wildlife sighting season runs from February to May.

Famous Historic Wildlife Event: Tiger T1C1's Epic Trek

Dnyanganga gained national prominence when a radio-collared sub-adult tiger, designated as T1C1, arrived at the sanctuary. Walking a record-breaking 1,300 km over six months from the Tipeshwar Wildlife Sanctuary in the Yavatmal district, it achieved what is recorded as the longest-known trek by a tiger in India.

Moving back and forth across farmlands, highways, and water bodies, T1C1's journey challenged traditional "source and sink" wildlife population theories and prompted conservationists and biologists like Bilal Habib from the Wildlife Institute of India to rethink conservation policies for dispersing tigers in non-protected corridors.

Access & Visitor Information

  • Entry Gate: The Botha Gate of the sanctuary is easily accessible by road from Khamgaon on the Khamgaon-Buldhana highway.
  • Nearest Railway Station: Shegaon railway station on the Nagpur-Mumbai main line.
